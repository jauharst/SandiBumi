Step by step: the run-to-run splice

The classic composite: one curve above the splice depth, the other at and below it. Two logging runs of the same tool, a casing shoe, a re-log over a washout. Wherever two deliveries of the same measurement meet at a depth, this is the join. Inputs are never modified; the result is a new _SPL curve.

The picks

GR above 1530 m, GR_EC at and below it: raw gamma up top, the hole-corrected version underneath, as a stand-in for a two-run delivery. The depth matters more than it looks: 1530 m sits inside all three wells' logged ranges (1495–1570 m), so both sides of the splice carry real samples. A splice depth outside the logged interval runs clean and demonstrates nothing: the output is silently one input end to end.

Run and read

On these wells: 3 clean, 1185 samples out, of which 606 below 1530 m carry the corrected curve and the rest the raw one. QC a real splice at the join: a step in the composite at the splice depth is the two runs disagreeing, and that disagreement is information about the runs, not something the splice should smooth over.

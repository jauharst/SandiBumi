Step by step: the quick-look pair

This module is a comparison shortcut, and its own manifest says so: the average and the gas-RMS of density and neutron porosity are field quick-looks, not a crossplot method. Use it to eyeball agreement between the two logs; for an actual analytic crossplot porosity go to Porosity from Bateman-Konen.

The refusal you will meet first

Run it on a freshly imported well and it refuses, by name:

neutron curve 'NPHI' has no DECLARED matrix basis. A limestone-unit neutron read against a sandstone matrix is ~0.04 v/v low in clean water sand, and an undeclared basis cannot be checked - declare it (set_curve_neutron_basis) or convert with nphimat first.

That is this run happening on camera: the SANDI deliveries say "limestone" in the LAS header, but the application never infers a matrix basis — it must be DECLARED (Curve Catalog → the neutron curve's basis, citing the header), or the curve converted with Neutron Matrix Conversion. Here the basis was declared LIMESTONE with the header as its source, and the run proceeded.

Enter the shale point

  • RHO_SH 2.45 / NPHI_SH 0.38 (limestone units, matching the declared curve) — medians of SANDI-01's samples at GR ≥ 112 gAPI. Everything else is a cited default, including the AVERAGE/GAS_RMS choice (AVERAGE).

Run and read

On these wells: 1 clean · 2 degraded. The degraded wells report two clamps — the shale-reduced neutron held to its [-0.015, 0.4] guard band at one sample, and the effective porosity held at the 0.001 floor at another. Single-sample clamps at guard rails are the normal texture of real logs; a well that failed outright would say failed and write nothing.

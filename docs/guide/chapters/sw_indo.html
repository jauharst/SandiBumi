What this is and when to reach for it

The Indonesia equation (Poupon and Leveaux, 1971): the field-standard shaly-sand saturation for fresh formation waters, built for exactly the kind of section this dataset imitates. It adds a shale conductivity path in parallel with the sand's, plus the cross term that makes the two interfere, so shaly intervals stop reading as water the way they do under Archie. If a study in a young clastic basin reports one resistivity saturation, it is usually this one.

The physics

1/RT = ( v/RT_SH + PHIE^M/(A·Rw)
        + 2·sqrt( v · PHIE^M / (A·Rw·RT_SH) ) ) · SW^N
v = VSH^(2−VSH)  (FULL)    v = VSH^2  (SIMPLE)    v = VSH^(2−2·VSH)  (TAR_SAND)

The equation works on effective porosity and takes the shale's own resistivity as a parameter, so the shale path is anchored to a measured number rather than an assumption. The exponent variant is part of the equation's identity: the three published forms weight the shale term differently, and the choice is declared, not tuned.

The picks and why

  • a 1, m 2, n 2: the reference-case convention, as the Archie chapter.
  • RW 0.058 ohm.m: the median wet-sand Rwa derived through effective porosity. In the Archie chapter that derivation clashed with the total-porosity identity; here it is the right pairing, because this is an effective-porosity equation.
  • RT_SH 2.5 ohm.m: the median deep resistivity of the shale population (GR ≥ 112, 55 samples), this dataset's own shale, not a book value.
  • SWE_IRR 0.16: the irreducible floor shared across the saturation book.

Step by step

  1. Run VSH from the Gamma Ray chapter and a porosity chapter; both resolve as inputs. The numbers below multiply the gamma-ray VSH, so if the density-neutron chapter's VSH is the one standing, re-run the gamma-ray one first.
  2. Read RT_SH off the shale population with the Inspector and Rw from a water zone.
  3. Open the Indonesia pane from Petrophysics ▸ Saturation, keep the FULL form unless the study argues otherwise, and press Run.

Reading the result

0 clean · 3 degraded: 92 or 93 samples per well computed below the 0.16 floor and were clamped-and-counted. Median SWE_INDO 0.6615. The populations tell the story: gas sand 0.0898, wet sand 0.7635. Against the effective-form Archie's wet-sand 0.8636 on identical picks, the shale term has moved even the cleanest rock by ten saturation units, because VSH is never quite zero there.

SELECT round(median(c.value), 4) FROM computed_curves c
JOIN standard_curves sc ON c.well_id = sc.well_id AND c.depth = sc.depth
WHERE c.curve_name = 'SWE_INDO' AND sc.gr < 60 AND sc.res_deep < 10
  AND NOT isnan(c.value)

The exponent variant, measured: SIMPLE on the same picks lifts the median to 0.6712. One saturation unit here, because v = VSH² feeds the shale path less conductivity than the FULL form at this section's shale volumes; sections with higher VSH separate further. The form is a published identity to declare in the methodology table, not a knob. Restored to FULL, every number returns exactly.

QC and pitfalls

  • Close the water-zone loop: 0.76 in the wet sand says the shale term and the Rw pick together slightly over-correct the cleanest rock here. Reading that check is how RT_SH and Rw get refined; not reading it is how a wrong pair ships.
  • RT_SH from another field's shale is the classic silent error; the number takes thirty seconds to measure on this dataset's own shale population.
  • The equation was published for fresh waters where the shale path matters most; in very saline brines the shale term loses its leverage and plain Archie with good m and n competes.

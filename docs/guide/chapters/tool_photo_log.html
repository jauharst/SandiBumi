What this is and when to reach for it

Photo Log reads curves off conditioned core photographs: darkness, redness and texture, sampled every couple of centimetres down the whole cored interval. That makes the photograph the densest measurement made on the core itself, since a plug table gives a few dozen samples where a photograph gives hundreds, and the traces it writes can anchor a depth registration, sit beside GR in a layout, or flag intervals worth a closer look. Reach for it after conditioning, never before.

The pane

  • The outputs are image measures, not petrophysical properties, and the pane leads with why:
Reads darkness, redness and texture down the core. They are IMAGE measures, not
petrophysical properties: darkness follows shale in most clastic sections without being
a shale volume, which is why nothing here is called VSH.
  • Depth runs across or down, with a "Deepest end first" toggle for a box laid out the other way round; Runs of core splits a multi-row box into equal lanes, and the column table handles a packed core-display plate whose barrels each carry their own depths, detected from the picture or typed from the caption, never guessed.
  • Check against correlates each trace with a real log before anything is written; Light: Ultraviolet switches the read to fluorescence bands for a UV delivery.

Step by step

  1. Open Advance ▸ Core Imaging ▸ Photo Log…, pick the conditioned delivery, set the lay-out, and choose GR under Check against.
  2. Read the trace and judge the signed agreement before saving anything.
  3. Save as curves (a custody dialog records who ran it and from what), then Build depth strips for the log-view track.

Reading the result

The example read 737 samples from 3 photographs over 1528.0–1542.9 m: against GR, DARK +0.91, RED +0.94, TEX −0.08. The sign is the QC: clay is dark and clay is radioactive, so darkness and GR should rise together, and a strongly negative agreement means the box was read the wrong way up, which a correlogram cannot tell from a genuine depth error, so the pane says it in words rather than proposing a shift. TEX staying near zero here is honest too: the lower unit's laminations run along the core, and texture measures spread across the core within each slab. Saving resampled the traces onto the well's own depth frame (395 samples, 99 of them inside the cored interval) by box average:

Written on the well's own depth frame (395 samples) so modules and plots can read them.
The photograph was read at about 0.020 - each stored sample is the AVERAGE of the
photograph samples inside it rather than one of them picked out, which would alias.

The strips land in their own CORE STRIP dataset, each box on its own interval, "so a gap between two runs stays a gap", and the example's 10 cm barrel gaps survive into the track. Rebuilding replaces the strips in place: a lane-count experiment leaves no STRIP_1, STRIP_2 trail behind.

QC and pitfalls

  • Calibrate before you quote. CPHOTO_DARK tracks shale in most clastic sections; turning it into a shale volume is a calibration you make against your own GR, which is why the curve refuses to be born as VSH.
  • An interval photograph only. A picture with no base depth is a point sample with no axis to read along; it is refused rather than stretched over a guessed thickness.
  • Depths outside the photographs stay empty. The saved curves are NaN off the cored interval, since no picture means no value, and a module reading them sees exactly where evidence exists.

Thin Beds — Thomas-Stieber

Module id thin_bed_ts · category ThinBeds

Thomas-Stieber decomposition of bulk shale into laminar and dispersed fractions, by placing the measured (VSH, PHIT) point against the pure-laminated and pure-dispersed mixing lines. Laminar shale reduces net sand; dispersed shale stays within the sand fraction. Structural shale is not modeled.

Pure laminated: PHIT = PHI_SD_MAX·(1−VSH) + PHI_SH·VSH
Pure dispersed: PHIT = PHI_SD_MAX − VSH·(1−PHI_SH)
VSAND = 1 − VLAM;  PHIE_LAM = laminar-corrected porosity of the net sand

Step by step: Thomas-Stieber

Thomas-Stieber places each measured (VSH, PHIT) point inside the mixing triangle between clean sand, pure laminated shale and pure dispersed shale, and reads off how much of the bulk shale is laminar (reducing net sand) and how much is dispersed (sitting inside the sand's pores). Structural shale is deliberately not modeled.

The two endpoints are field statements

  • PHI_SD_MAX 0.27: the clean-sand maximum porosity, the same P99-of-PHIE pick the porosity-ceiling chapter derived from this dataset.
  • PHI_SH 0.147: the shale total porosity, the median PHIT_DEN over the samples at GR ≥ 112 gAPI, this dataset's own shale point.
  • Inputs: PHIT_DEN and VSH, so the triangle is built from the same porosity family the endpoints were picked from. Mixing porosity provenances here moves points around the triangle for reasons that have nothing to do with shale.

Run and read

On these wells: 0 clean · 3 degraded, about 120 samples per well clamped to [0, 1] (a measured point falling outside the mixing triangle cannot yield a fraction beyond it, so the decomposition is held at the triangle's edge) and a handful held at the PHI_SD_MAX ceiling. In the transitional interval (GR 60–100) the median laminar fraction reads 0.635: this synthetic section's shale transitions read as predominantly laminated, which fits zone-blended logs. On real thin-bedded rock, VLAM against a high-resolution image log is the validation this decomposition wants before VSAND = 1−VLAM revises any net.

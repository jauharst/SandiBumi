## Methods
### Passey delta-log-R (sonic-, density-, and neutron-resistivity variants)
Curve-overlay TOC estimator. A porosity log and a deep resistivity log are scale-normalized and baselined in a non-source shale; in organic-rich intervals the curves separate (delta-log-R), and the separation converts to TOC given thermal maturity (LOM). Source: Passey et al. 1990 AAPG (NOT in library; workflow + TOC equation confirmed in-library in Rider pp.104-105, Figs 6.38/6.39/8.23). Physics: kerogen slows sonic / lowers density / raises apparent neutron porosity regardless of maturity; resistivity only rises when maturity generates free HC in pores — so immature source shows porosity-curve deflection without resistivity deflection.

Equations:
Sonic variant: dlogR = log10(Rt/Rt_base) + 0.02*(DT - DT_base)   [DT in us/ft; 0.02 = 1/50, i.e. 50 us/ft per resistivity decade — the exact scaling Rider p.104 quotes: '50 us/ft = 1 logarithmic cycle, e.g. 0.01-0.1 ohmm']. Density variant: dlogR = log10(Rt/Rt_base) - 2.50*(RHOB - RHOB_base)   [2.50 = 1/0.4, 0.4 g/cc per decade]. Neutron variant: dlogR = log10(Rt/Rt_base) + 4.00*(NPHI - NPHI_base)   [4.00 = 1/0.25, 25 p.u. per decade]. TOC (all variants): TOC_wt% = dlogR * 10^(2.297 - 0.1688*LOM). Metric sonic: coefficient 0.02/0.3048 if DT in us/m (keep us/ft internally). Set dlogR<0 to 0.

Inputs: RT (deep resistivity, ohmm), DT (us/ft) and/or RHOB (g/cc) and/or NPHI (v/v); per-zone baseline pairs (Rt_base with DT_base/RHOB_base/NPHI_base); LOM (level of organic metamorphism, Hood et al. 1975; practical range ~6-12), usually derived from vitrinite reflectance Ro or RockEval Tmax; optional GR/RHOB/PEF cutoffs to mask coals and hydrocarbon reservoirs (both give false delta-log-R separation — Rider p.104: 'eliminated on lithological grounds using, for example, the gamma ray').
Outputs: DLR (curve separation, log10 resistivity decades) per variant; TOC_wt% per variant plus a combined/preferred TOC; QC flags for coal/reservoir exclusion zones; optionally S2/HI via RockEval regression (see calibration module).
Calibration: Baseline picking workflow (the key UI feature): (1) display resistivity on log scale overlaid with porosity curve on inverted linear scale locked at the variant's decades-per-unit ratio; (2) user drags/picks a depth interval of organic-lean ('non-source') shale where the two curves track each other over tens of metres within one compaction/stratigraphic regime; app reads averaged baseline values there; (3) re-baseline per zone/well (baselines shift with compaction, unconformities, borehole condition). LOM: build a Ro->LOM (and Tmax->Ro via Ro_calc = 0.0180*Tmax - 7.16, Jarvie) lookup digitized from Hood et al. 1975; if core TOC exists, bypass LOM by regressing measured TOC vs dlogR to fit TOC = A*dlogR (+B). Validation: cross-plot predicted vs core (LECO/RockEval) TOC, report R2/RMSE. Known bias: overestimates at low maturity, underestimates when over-mature (LOM>~10.5) — motivates the modified variants.

### Modified Passey variants (generalized refit; Wang 2016 over-mature; Zhao optimized-coefficient)
Family of delta-log-R modifications for cases where original Passey coefficients fail (over-mature marine shales, lacustrine shales, high-carbonate mudrocks). None of the source papers are in Jauhar's library — spec is structural, from standard literature knowledge; exact published coefficients must be pulled from the papers (Wang et al. 2016; Zhao et al.) or, better, refit to local core, which is the recommended SandiBumi implementation.

Equations:
Generalized calibrated form (implement this one; it subsumes all variants): dlogR_gen = log10(Rt/Rt_base) + K*(P - P_base) where P is the chosen porosity log and K is a user/fit scaling factor (defaults 0.02, -2.5, 4.0); TOC = a * dlogR_gen * 10^(b - c*MAT) + d, with MAT = LOM or Ro (user choice). Passey defaults: a=1, b=2.297, c=0.1688, MAT=LOM, d=0. Wang et al. 2016 (over-mature shale) structure: replaces LOM with measured Ro and refits (a,b,c,K) to over-mature core data, and treats the scaling factor K as maturity-dependent rather than fixed; Zhao-type variants: optimize overlay coefficient K and baseline per interval by regression against core TOC (removes subjective baseline picking) and may add a background/inert-carbon offset d. All reduce to: nonlinear least squares of (a,b,c,K,d) on core TOC.

Inputs: Same logs as Passey; Ro (measured or from Tmax); core TOC set for fitting; choice of variant/porosity curve; per-zone fit domains.
Outputs: TOC_wt% with locally fitted coefficients; stored coefficient sets per zone/field (SandiBumi should persist these as named calibrations, like Geolog paysum specs); fit diagnostics (R2, RMSE, residual-vs-depth).
Calibration: Fit coefficients by regression on RockEval/LECO core TOC (depth-shifted). Guard rails: require >=10-15 core points spanning the TOC range; hold out points for validation; warn when applying a fit outside its Ro range. Note for notes[]: exact Wang 2016 / Zhao published coefficient values intentionally not quoted — not in library, do not fabricate.

### Schmoker density method (incl. Myers & Jenkyns 1992 volumetric density TOC — full equations in-library)
TOC from bulk density alone: organic matter (rho ~0.9-1.2 g/cc) is much lighter than clay matrix (~2.7 g/cc), so density drops nearly linearly with organic content. Schmoker (1979, Appalachian Devonian; 1981) formalized this; Rider p.126 documents the approach and its caveats (pyrite masks the effect — Passey et al. abandoned density partly for this; compaction must be accounted for; barite mud invalidates). Rider p.127 gives the complete Myers & Jenkyns (1992) two-interval volumetric formulation, which needs no long normalization: subtract a contiguous non-source interval's log response.

Equations:
General Schmoker form: phi_org = (rho_ns - rho_b)/(rho_ns - rho_org) [volume fraction organic matter, rho_ns = organic-lean shale bulk density from adjacent non-source interval]; TOC_wt% = 100 * C * phi_org * rho_org / rho_b, where C ~0.75-0.85 = carbon weight fraction of organic matter (Schmoker used OM/TOC ratio ~1.3). Myers & Jenkyns 1992 (verbatim from Rider p.127, eqs 1-3): (1) phi_fl = (rho_ns - rho_ma)/(rho_fl - rho_ma); (2) phi_ker = (rho_s - rho_ns)/(rho_ker - rho_ma); (3) TOC% = 0.85*rho_ker*phi_ker / [rho_ker*phi_ker + rho_ma*(1 - phi_fl - phi_ker)]. Defaults (Rider): rho_ma = 2.70 g/cc mudrock matrix, rho_fl = 1.05 g/cc water, rho_ker = 1.1 or 1.2 g/cc, TOC = 0.85 * wt% kerogen. rho_ns = averaged density of contiguous non-source interval (from log), rho_s = density in source interval (from log).

Inputs: RHOB (g/cc); picked non-source reference interval (same UI gesture as Passey baseline); rho_ma, rho_fl, rho_ker parameters; pyrite/siderite flag logs (PEF or manual) to mask; compaction-trend option (depth-dependent rho_ns).
Outputs: TOC_wt%, kerogen volume fraction phi_ker, water-filled porosity phi_fl; masked-interval flags.
Calibration: Normalize density log to sample analyses (Schmoker & Hester 1983 approach per Rider p.126: regress core TOC vs log density in the same well/basin, then interpolate between analysed points and export to un-cored wells). Assumptions to surface in UI: constant matrix density and water-filled porosity between source and non-source intervals; fails with variable pyrite (rho 4.8-5.17) or siderite (3.0-3.89) or carbonate cement.

### Schmoker-Hester density TOC (Bakken 1983 inverse form)
The widely used single-equation inverse-density TOC, published for the Bakken by Schmoker & Hester (1983). Equation confirmed in-library: Rider Fig 9.23 caption (p.126) prints it with the Bakken core-comparison crossplot.

Equations:
TOC_wt% = (154.497 / RHOB) - 57.261  [RHOB in g/cc; Bakken Fm, Williston Basin coefficients]. Generalized refit form for other basins: TOC = A/RHOB - B (fit A, B to core), which is the hyperbolic equivalent of the linear Schmoker mix model with fixed rho_org (~1.01 g/cc used originally).

Inputs: RHOB only (borehole-quality gated: caliper/DRHO cutoff; barite-free mud required); optional zone limits to organic shale only.
Outputs: TOC_wt% continuous curve; recommended clip 0-40 wt%.
Calibration: Refit A and B by regressing core TOC vs 1/RHOB per formation. QC per Rider: dashed-line ideal-agreement crossplot of density-TOC vs core-TOC (Fig 9.23 style plot should be a built-in QC display). Reject in pyritic intervals and washouts.

### Uranium / gamma-ray TOC correlation (incl. excess-uranium Th/U method)
TOC from spectral-GR uranium (or total GR where spectral absent): marine organic matter adsorbs uranium from seawater, so U (ppm) correlates with TOC in marine anoxic source rocks. In-library support: Rider pp.81-82 (Fig 7.22 organic carbon vs total GR correlation; clay is a catalyst for adsorption; coals are LOW GR), pp.88-89 (Kimmeridge TOC>5% high U; Myers & Wignall 1987 excess-uranium; Th/U environment logic). Critical caveat captured in Rider p.88: lacustrine source rocks have NO uranium enrichment (Meyer & Nederlof 1983) — gamma/U methods only for marine source rocks; important for Mahakam deltaic Type-III organic matter where this method is expected to perform poorly.

Equations:
Linear calibration: TOC_wt% = a*U + b (U in ppm from HNGS/NGT), or TOC = a*GR + b against total/computed GR (HSGR/HCGR); fit a,b on core TOC. Excess uranium (Myers & Wignall 1987, verbatim logic Rider p.89): U_norm = Th_log/3 [normal shale Th/U = 3, cf. measured 3.9+-0.7 Kimmeridge, 3.8+-1.1 Adams & Weaver]; U_excess = U_log - U_norm; correlate TOC with U_excess instead of raw U (removes detrital-clay uranium). Interpretation cutoffs: Th/U < 3 => uranium-enriched/anoxic, potential source; Th/U > 7 => leached/oxidized.

Inputs: U, Th, K from spectral GR (ppm, ppm, %); or total GR (API); core TOC for regression; marine/lacustrine facies flag (disable or warn for non-marine).
Outputs: TOC_wt%, U_excess curve, Th/U curve (also useful as condensed-section/anoxia indicator track).
Calibration: Per-well or per-field OLS of core TOC vs U (or U_excess); check phosphate/unconformity U spikes (exclude — U unrelated to TOC there, Rider p.83); verify coals excluded (low GR but very high TOC would wreck the regression).

### Meyer & Nederlof (1984) source-rock discriminant
Screening classifier (not a TOC quantifier): a discriminant function of sonic and temperature-corrected resistivity separates source from non-source rock (shale AND limestone) — derived from worldwide source-rock analysis. Exact sonic-resistivity equation in-library (Rider p.104, Fig 8.22 shows the D=0 line on the dt vs R75F crossplot).

Equations:
D = -6.906 + 3.186*log10(DT) + 0.487*log10(R75)  [DT = sonic, us/ft; R75 = resistivity corrected to 75 F (24 C)]. D > 0 => source rock; D < 0 => non-source. Temperature correction (Arps): R75 = R_T * (T_F + 6.77)/(75 + 6.77) with T_F formation temp in F (or (T_C + 21.5)/(24 + 21.5) in C) — needs the formation temperature module. The original paper (Meyer & Nederlof 1984, Geologie en Mijnbouw / AAPG) also contains a density-resistivity discriminant variant; coefficients not in library — obtain paper before implementing that variant, do not guess.

Inputs: DT (us/ft), RT (ohmm), formation temperature (from geothermal gradient module); optionally RHOB for second variant later.
Outputs: D curve and binary/ternary source-rock flag track; crossplot view dt vs R75 with D=0 line (mirrors Rider Fig 8.22).
Calibration: None required (fixed published coefficients) — but validate flag against intervals of known core TOC >= ~1.5-2 wt%; if systematically wrong, fall back to refitting the three coefficients by linear discriminant analysis on local labeled data.

### Multi-linear regression / ML TOC from logs
Direct data-driven TOC: regress core TOC against multiple log curves — the pragmatic default when maturity data are missing or facies are mixed (and the natural companion to SandiBumi's DuckDB storage: fit and store models per field). Library support is generic only (ML review PDFs); method is standard practice.

Equations:
MLR: TOC = b0 + b1*GR + b2*DT + b3*RHOB + b4*NPHI + b5*log10(RT) (+ b6*U + b7*PEF as available); fit by OLS (optionally ridge/lasso for collinearity — GR/NPHI/RHOB are strongly correlated). Optional engineered features: dlogR itself as a predictor, 1/RHOB, Th/U. ML tier: gradient-boosted trees or random forest on the same feature set (recommended over deep nets at core-TOC sample sizes, typically 20-200 points); k-fold CV grouped BY WELL (never random by-sample — depth autocorrelation leaks; the 2024 C&G ML review in-library documents these pitfalls).

Inputs: Any subset of GR, DT, RHOB, NPHI, RT, U, Th, K, PEF (environmental-corrected, depth-aligned, resampled to common increment); core TOC table (depth, TOC_wt%, method LECO/RockEval); train-domain zone limits.
Outputs: TOC_wt% curve + prediction interval (e.g. from quantile regression forest or OLS confidence band); stored model object (coefficients or serialized tree ensemble) with metadata (wells used, feature list, fit stats); feature-importance report.
Calibration: Workflow: depth-shift core to logs (cross-correlate core GR if available), remove bad-hole samples (caliper/DRHO gate), fit, blind-well validation with R2/RMSE, residual-vs-depth QC plot. Persist calibrations per formation; warn on extrapolation outside training feature ranges.

### RockEval / LECO calibration & derived geochemical outputs (S1, S2, HI, maturity conversions)
Supporting module: ingest RockEval pyrolysis and LECO TOC core tables, drive calibration of all log-TOC methods, and extend log TOC to S2/HI/oil-in-place style outputs. Kennedy 2.9 (in-library) covers pyrolysis conceptually; equations are standard geochemistry (literature).

Equations:
Definitions: S1 = free HC (mg HC/g rock), S2 = pyrolyzable HC from kerogen cracking (mg HC/g rock), S3 = organic CO2 (mg/g), Tmax (C) at S2 peak. Indices: HI = 100*S2/TOC (mg HC/g TOC); OI = 100*S3/TOC; PI = S1/(S1+S2). Maturity: Ro_calc(%) = 0.0180*Tmax - 7.16 (Jarvie; feeds LOM lookup for Passey). Log-derived S2: regress core S2 vs core TOC per kerogen population, S2 = k*(TOC - TOC_inert), apply k and TOC_inert to log TOC curve; then HI_log = 100*S2_log/TOC_log. Oil saturation index: OSI = 100*S1/TOC; OSI > 100 mg HC/g TOC = 'oil crossover', producible-oil indicator (Jarvie). Screening cutoffs to build into UI: TOC < 0.5 poor, 0.5-1 fair, 1-2 good, 2-4 very good, > 4 excellent source; HI > ~300 oil-prone (Type I/II), 50-200 gas-prone (Type III), < 50 inert (Type IV).

Inputs: Core tables: depth, TOC (LECO and/or RockEval), S1, S2, S3, Tmax, Ro measurements; log TOC from any method above.
Outputs: Calibration fits + QC crossplots (pred vs measured TOC; S2 vs TOC; HI vs Tmax pseudo-Van-Krevelen with kerogen-type fields); continuous S2_log, HI_log, OSI curves; Ro/LOM curve for Passey input.
Calibration: This module IS the calibration layer: every TOC method registers its predictions against this core table; store per-zone regression coefficients in DuckDB with provenance (wells, date, n, R2).

### Brittleness index (mineralogical + elastic)
Standard unconventional completion-quality outputs. No reference papers in library (Geomechanics folder is unreadable pptx field reports) — spec'd from standard literature: Jarvie et al. 2007 / Wang & Gale 2009 (mineralogical), Rickman et al. 2008 SPE 115258 (log-elastic). Feeds naturally from SandiBumi's multimin mineral volumes and sonic outputs.

Equations:
Mineralogical (Jarvie 2007): BI_min = Qz / (Qz + Carb + Clay) [weight or volume fractions from multimin/XRD]. Wang & Gale 2009 variant: BI = (Qz + Dol) / (Qz + Dol + Cal + Clay + TOC). Elastic (Rickman 2008, dynamic from DTC/DTS/RHOB): E_brit = 100*(E - 1)/(8 - 1) with E = dynamic Young's modulus in Mpsi (10^6 psi); PR_brit = 100*(nu - 0.4)/(0.15 - 0.4); BI_elastic = (E_brit + PR_brit)/2. Dynamic moduli: nu = (0.5*(DTS/DTC)^2 - 1)/((DTS/DTC)^2 - 1); G = a*RHOB/DTS^2; E = 2G(1+nu) (unit-consistent constants per us/ft + g/cc inputs).

Inputs: Mineral volume curves (from multimin module) and/or TOC; DTC, DTS, RHOB for elastic variant.
Outputs: BI_min, BI_elastic (0-100%), combined/averaged BI, brittle-vs-ductile zonation flags for completion-interval ranking.
Calibration: Optional recalibration of Rickman min/max normalization anchors (1-8 Mpsi E; 0.40-0.15 nu) to local field ranges; sanity-check dynamic-to-static conversion if core geomechanics available.

### Adsorbed / free gas in place (shale gas & CBM basics)
Basic gas-in-place accounting for organic shale/CBM: adsorbed gas via Langmuir isotherm (TOC-scaled), free gas via porosity-saturation volumetrics, with optional Ambrose correction for pore volume occupied by the adsorbed phase. Library support: Kennedy 2.9/3.9 (adsorption concept, crushed-core measurement); CBM course pptx (unreadable) in PetroSkills folder; equations standard literature (Langmuir; Ambrose et al. 2012).

Equations:
Adsorbed: Gc_ads (scf/ton) = VL * P / (PL + P), with Langmuir volume VL (scf/ton) and pressure PL (psia) from core isotherms; TOC scaling for un-cored intervals: VL(z) = VL_ref * TOC(z)/TOC_ref (isotherms are strongly TOC-proportional); apply lab->reservoir temperature correction if isotherm T differs. Free: Gc_free (scf/ton) = 32.1052 * phi_e * (1 - Sw) / (rho_b * Bg) [phi_e v/v, rho_b g/cc, Bg res-cf/scf; constant converts g/cc-v/v to scf/ton]. Ambrose correction (optional): reduce phi available to free gas by adsorbed-phase volume: phi_free = phi_e*(1-Sw) - (1.318e-6 * M / rho_ads) * Gc_ads * rho_b (M = gas molar mass g/mol, rho_ads ~0.34-0.42 g/cc); document as toggle. Totals: GIP (Bcf) = 1.3597e-6 * A(acres) * h(ft) * rho_b * (Gc_ads + Gc_free) style summation, or per-foot Gc curves summed over net intervals.

Inputs: TOC curve (from any method above), phi_e, Sw, RHOB, pressure & temperature profiles (gradients), Bg from gas properties (needs z-factor subroutine), Langmuir VL/PL (+isotherm T, TOC_ref) from core.
Outputs: Gc_ads, Gc_free, Gc_total (scf/ton) curves; cumulative GIP per zone; adsorbed fraction track.
Calibration: Langmuir parameters from crushed-core canister/isotherm lab data (Kennedy 3.9 documents the crushed-core protocol); calibrate TOC-scaling of VL by plotting core VL vs core TOC; validate total against canister desorption gas contents where available.

Duplicate files noted: two copies of the 2006 Passey thin-bed paper and two of Spooner 2014 exist in the reference folder root.
